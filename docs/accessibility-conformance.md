# accessibility conformance record

## what is claimed

netdash toolkit targets **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**. it does not fully meet that target today. of the 55 Level A and Level AA success criteria in [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), being 31 at Level A and 24 at Level AA once the obsolete 4.1.1 parsing is removed, this evaluation records **32 as supported, 14 as partially supported, and 9 as not applicable**, with 0 recorded as wholly unsupported.

the 14 partial verdicts collapse into 11 distinct defects, listed as gaps 1 through 11 below, because three of them share a single root cause.

the honest summary is that the claim is not yet true. the gaps are concentrated in three places: error messages that are not programmatically tied to the fields that produced them, asynchronous results that are not announced, and a small number of controls below the 24 by 24 css pixel target floor. none of them is architectural, and each has a named remediation below.

this document follows the shape of an accessibility conformance report. it is written to be read by someone who intends to check it, so every verdict names the evidence behind it and every limitation is stated next to the claim it limits rather than collected into a disclaimer at the end.

## scope

**what was evaluated:** the web build at commit-time `HEAD`, comprising the dashboard, the 48 tool routes under `/tools/[slug]`, `/projects`, `/about`, and the unauthenticated `/auth/action` route used for password reset and email verification links.

**what was not evaluated:** the Electron desktop build. it renders the same react tree from the same static export, so the component-level findings here carry over, but its window chrome, native menu bar, and focus behaviour on window activation are supplied by Electron rather than by this codebase and were not assessed. no claim is made for the desktop build.

**baseline:** the evaluation assumes a current browser with javascript enabled. the app is a static export with no server-rendered fallback, so it does not function without javascript, which is a product decision rather than a conformance one, since WCAG does not require it.

three methods, in descending order of how much they prove.

**static analysis of source and stylesheet.** several criteria have no runtime signature in a test environment without layout. those were assessed by resolving tailwind classes through `tailwind-merge`, the same library `cn()` uses at runtime, so a caller's `h-4` overriding a base `h-8` resolves in the analysis exactly as it does in the browser, and by reading `app/globals.css` directly.

**code reading.** the shared primitives in `components/ui/`, the application shell, and the authentication flows were read in full.

### what this evaluation could not do, and what that costs

**no browser was used.** the test environment is happy-dom, which has no layout engine, no paint, and no viewport. this evaluation therefore could not observe a single rendered pixel. the practical consequences, criterion by criterion:

- **1.4.3 and 1.4.11 contrast** are computed from the hex values of the design tokens in `app/globals.css`, not sampled from painted pixels. this is sound where a foreground token sits on the surface it was designed for, and it does not cover text composited over a translucent layer, over an image, or over a gradient. the codebase uses none of those, which is why the token-level result is meaningful, but it is a narrower claim than a pixel sample would be.
- **1.4.10 reflow** was assessed by looking for the authoring decisions that cause it to fail: fixed widths above 320 css pixels, containers that clip instead of scroll, tables with no horizontal scroll ancestor. no page was actually rendered at 320 css pixels. a layout that reflows badly for a reason not on that list would not have been caught.
- **1.4.4 resize text** and **1.4.12 text spacing** were assessed the same way, by confirming nothing in the stylesheet would win against a user override and that no px font sizes are declared in css. neither was observed at 200% zoom or with the 1.4.12 override stylesheet applied.
- **2.4.11 focus not obscured** was resolved by reading the layout tree rather than by tabbing through a rendered page, which is stated in full in the row for that criterion because the conclusion depends entirely on that reading being correct.

**no screen reader was used.** nothing here reports how NVDA, JAWS, or VoiceOver actually announce this interface. the aria in the tree was checked for correctness, and correct aria is a precondition for a good announcement rather than a guarantee of one. the criteria whose real-world outcome cannot be known without a screen reader are 1.3.1, 1.3.2, 2.4.6, 3.3.1, 4.1.2, and 4.1.3, and each is marked accordingly.

**no assistive technology other than a screen reader was considered.** speech input, switch access, and screen magnification were not exercised. 2.5.3 is the criterion most relevant to speech input and it is tested statically across all 48 tools, but a static match is not the same as a successful voice command.

## known gaps and remediation status

ordered by how much they affect a real user, not by how easy they are to fix. none is owned by the shared primitives, which is why none was fixed in this pass.

**gap 1. asynchronous results and validation errors are not announced or associated.** the largest gap, and it spans two criteria: 3.3.1 and 4.1.3, with 3.3.3 riding on it. around 12 tools compute a result asynchronously and render it, plus any error, into a region with no `aria-live`, and tool-level validation messages are rendered as detached banners rather than being tied to the offending field with `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`. a screen reader user submits a form and hears nothing. remediation: wrap each result surface in `role="status"`, and give each validation message an `id` referenced by the field's `aria-describedby` with `aria-invalid` set. the pattern to copy already exists in `components/ui/ip-input.tsx:105-124`. **status: open, owned by the tool workstreams.**

**gap 2. two password fields have no autocomplete token.** `app/auth/action/page.tsx` sets the new password during a reset. affects 1.3.5 and 3.3.8. remediation: add `autoComplete="new-password"` to both fields. the equivalent 13 fields in `components/ui/` were fixed this cycle. **status: open, one-line fix, outside the primitives.**

**gap 3. `/auth/action` has no descriptive title.** it serves password reset, email verification, and email recovery, and reports the generic site title for all three. affects 2.4.2. remediation: add `app/auth/action/layout.tsx` exporting `metadata`, since the page itself is a client component and cannot. **status: open.**

**gap 4. two `role="button"` elements respond to Enter but not Space.** `components/project-manager.tsx:511` and `:566`. affects 2.1.1. remediation: handle both keys, or make them real buttons. **status: open, capped at two by `tests/unit/wcag-keyboard-operability.test.ts` so no third can be added unnoticed.**

**gap 5. a bare `/` opens the command palette.** `components/command-palette.tsx:75`. affects 2.1.4. it is guarded against firing while the user is typing in a field, which removes the common failure, but the criterion requires the shortcut be switchable off, remappable, or active only when a component has focus, and none of those is true. remediation: drop the bare `/` and keep `Cmd/Ctrl+K`, which is modified and therefore out of scope, or add a preference to disable it. **status: open.**

**gap 6. at least one status is conveyed by colour alone.** `components/tools/network-tester/mtu-panel.tsx`. affects 1.4.1. remediation: add a text or icon indicator alongside the colour. **status: open.**

**gap 7. three live clocks cannot be paused.** `timestamp-converter.tsx:79`, `jwt-decoder.tsx:62`, `cron-parser.tsx:67`. affects 2.2.2. a case can be made that live updating is essential to a "current timestamp" display, which would make the exception apply, but that case is not established and this record does not lean on it. remediation: add a pause control, or document the essential-exception argument deliberately. **status: open, verdict deliberately conservative.**

**gap 8. one button's name does not contain its visible label.** `components/tools/dns-tools.tsx` shows "Clear cache" and is named "Clear DNS cache". affects 2.5.3. remediation: make the visible text and the accessible name agree in either direction. **status: open, pinned by an explicit entry in `tests/components/wcag-label-in-name.test.tsx` so it cannot be lost.**

**gap 9. arbitrary px type does not respond to text-only resize.** fifteen `text-[10px]` and `text-[11px]` utilities at the time of writing, spread across `components/command-palette.tsx`, `components/dashboard.tsx`, `components/header.tsx`, and four tool files. affects 1.4.4 on the text-resize pathway only; browser zoom still scales them. that count is approximate on purpose, because those files are being rewritten concurrently and the figure moved from ten to fifteen during this evaluation. remediation: use a rem value such as `text-[0.625rem]`. **status: open. the shared primitives carry a hard floor of zero in `tests/unit/wcag-reflow-and-spacing.test.ts`, and the rest is held under a loose ceiling rather than an exact count for the reason above.**

**gap 10. the light-theme menu highlight measures 2.54:1.** `--accent` on `--popover` in `app/globals.css`. affects 1.4.11 for the focused row of a dropdown or select. the focused row's text colour changes at the same time and stays at 7.04:1, which mitigates it, but the boundary between the focused and unfocused row is below 3:1 on its own. the dark theme measures 5.77:1 and passes. remediation: darken `--accent` in the light theme, or use `--primary` at 5.48:1 for the highlight. this was left alone deliberately because it is a visual design decision across every menu in the app and could not be reviewed in a browser. **status: open, measured and pinned in `tests/unit/wcag-state-contrast.test.ts`, which fails if the value moves in either direction.**

**gap 11. four controls remain under 24 by 24 css pixels.** two copy buttons at 16 pixels in `components/tools/conflict-checker/analysis.tsx:56,63`, one at 20 pixels in `components/tools/reference-hub/reference-table.tsx:96`, and the "Cancel and return to app" button at 20 pixels in `app/auth/action/page.tsx:282`. affects 2.5.8. the last of those is a link-styled button standing alone in its own centred block rather than sitting inside a sentence, so the inline exception does not rescue it. remediation: drop the `h-4 w-4 p-0` and `h-5 w-5 p-0` overrides and let the button primitive supply its own size, which already clears the floor, and give the auth button vertical padding. **status: open, ceilinged in `tests/unit/wcag-target-size.test.ts`.**

## what to conclude from this document, and what not to

**you may conclude** that the mechanically detectable failures are absent across all 48 tools and stay absent, because axe runs on every one of them on every change rather than as a one-off audit; that the criteria axe cannot see have been reasoned about individually rather than waved through; that where a gap exists it is named with a file, a line, and a fix; and that the shared primitives, which is where a single defect reaches all 48 tools at once, have been read in full and corrected.

**you may not conclude** that this product conforms to WCAG 2.2 Level AA today. eleven criteria are partially supported and the largest of them, gap 1, affects a screen reader user's ability to know that anything happened at all when they use a tool. a conformance claim would be false until at least gaps 1 through 5 are closed.

**you may not conclude** that the supported verdicts have been confirmed against real assistive technology. they have not. no screen reader was run, no browser was opened, and no page was rendered at any viewport. the contrast figures are computed from design tokens rather than sampled from pixels, the reflow and text-spacing verdicts are inferences from source rather than observations of layout, and the 2.4.11 verdict is a reading of a layout tree. each of those is a defensible method and none of them is a substitute for looking.

**the remaining work that genuinely requires a human** is a screen reader pass on a representative sample of tools covering 1.3.1, 2.4.6, 3.3.1, 4.1.2, and 4.1.3; a zoom and reflow pass at 320 css pixels and at 200% covering 1.4.4, 1.4.10, and 1.4.12; a keyboard pass through the mobile drawer and the command palette on a touch device; and a colour judgement on gap 10, which is a design decision rather than a technical one.
